## 1. Objective and constraints

**Objective.** Forty founding families with a first Hidden Report Card delivered by
**31 January 2027**, so that every family has had at least one natural reason to return
(a report card) before the evidence checkpoint is read in **March 2027**.

**Constraints.**

- Capacity is not the constraint: 10+ per month covers forty in five months with room.
  Every report is still produced by hand until the engine (F03) exists; the plan must
  never sell faster than the five-business-day promise can be kept.
- Trust from strangers is the constraint. Every family so far knew the founder. The site
  today asks a stranger to pay $597 for an artefact they cannot see, with no proof on the
  page.
- The evidence checkpoint is behavioural, not temporal. Filling forty in one burst is worse
  than filling them across the calendar, because the checkpoint needs *return opportunities*,
  and those come from the school year.
- No referral machinery, no behavioural nudges (ratified). Both contaminate the signals the
  checkpoint reads.

**What this is not.** Not a brand strategy, not a twelve-month growth plan, not a content
business. It is a launch plan for one cohort, with the measurement that tells you whether
to build V2.

## 2. Who

### The early adopter

**The parent who already writes things down about her child.** The one who keeps a strategy
book in ChatGPT for an ADHD daughter, who feeds report cards to an AI before a school
meeting, who sat down unasked and wrote three pages of her daughter's strengths. She has
built a workaround, which means the pain is real and the habit of contributing already
exists. She is the person who makes the second exchange.

Not "wealthy families." Wealth governs who can afford it; this governs who to find first.

### Two cohorts, deliberately

| Cohort | Seats | Who | Experience | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Founding** | ~30 | Network and word of mouth; people who know the founder or were referred by someone who does | Founding as advertised — co-design access, direct contact, the founder's attention | Fill fast, generate proof, seed referral |
| **Clean** | ~10 | Referred strangers and social-content arrivals whom the founder does not know | The standard product: in-app, five business days, no phone number, the honest wait status | The experiment: does a parent who does not know Jolene pay, and return unprompted within eight weeks? |

The split is the point. Without the clean ten, the checkpoint cannot distinguish *the
picture is worth feeding* from *Jolene is worth talking to.* The clean cohort converts more
slowly and is worth the seats — its output is the one fact the business model rests on.

**The two geographies map onto the two cohorts almost by themselves.** New Brunswick is
where the network is: founding. South Florida arrives mostly through someone else's network —
the American collaborator's — and those parents do not know the founder: clean by
construction. (The founder is herself based in South Florida part of the year, per her
LinkedIn headline; parents she meets there in person are founding, not clean, and are the
referral seed for the clean ten.)
This is a proposal, not a rule (D-2); it means the stranger experiment also tests a US buyer,
which is the market the USD price was written for.

Tracking for ten people is a list the founder keeps, not a feature. Attribution at
registration (S03, built) already records how each family arrived; the cohort label sits
beside it in the founder's own record.

## 3. The offer

**Sell the year, not the report.** The value record's §3: the unit of value is the exchange,
and continuation is the count of exchanges. The homepage's middle already says this ("a
living document," "keep adding, and regenerate whenever you want"). The hero, the product
name and the button say the opposite. §6 fixes that.

**Founding means co-design.** For the founding thirty, say what it is: the first families,
reports reviewed personally, a voice in what gets built, direct contact with the founder.
That is the Inner Circle offer already on the site, given to the people who are actually
in it. The stale Inner Circle tier should either become this or leave the homepage
(decision D-3, §9).

**Clean means the product.** For the clean ten, the promise is the platform's: upload, five
business days, read in-app, add what you notice, regenerate. No founder access in the copy.
The wait status stays honest ("being reviewed personally before release") because that is
what happens.

**FOUNDING40, honestly.** 40% off the first year and 20% off renewals is already published;
`website-compliance-remediation-spec.md` D2 says the two-coupon promise cannot ship as
written in Stripe and names the fix. That decision is the founder's and predates this plan.
The strategy assumes whichever branch she takes; what it needs is that the *published*
promise and the *charged* amount match before the first stranger pays.

**Currency and language.** Two markets, two currencies. South Florida makes the USD price
deliberate rather than accidental — the audit's concern ("an American price tag that reads
as domestic") now applies only to the New Brunswick half. Compliance D1 therefore narrows to:
*add a CAD price for Canadian buyers*, owned by the founder; the currency picker already on
the pricing card is the surface for it. The founding families are francophone New
Brunswickers who use the product in English; the French site is not on this cohort's path.

**Refunds and jurisdiction.** D5 is owned by the founder and blocks the terms page; no
stranger campaign runs until terms are live. The founding cohort can proceed on the existing
relationship. **Selling into Florida adds a US consumer to documents drafted for Canada:**
federal auto-renewal rules (ROSCA), Florida's own auto-renewal provisions, and COPPA for the
children's data — the platform is already COPPA-aware, but the privacy policy and terms have
not been reviewed for a US buyer. That is a counsel item (`app/legal/COUNSEL-BRIEFING.md`),
and it sits on the clean cohort's critical path, not the founding cohort's.

## 4. The message

### 4.0 The StoryBrand script (added 2026-09-06; rebuilt the same day from the founder's own answers)

Donald Miller's frame, applied so every bio, hero and caption keeps the parent as the hero and
the founder as the guide. The rows below are the founder's words from a five-question
interview on 2026-09-06, lightly compressed; where a row is mine, it says so.

**The founder's answers, verbatim where it matters:**

1. *What a stranger should walk away understanding:* "I can help them do something with what
   they already see in their child: preserve it, bring it into conversations with school, and
   make sure grades aren't the only evidence they have to speak for their child."
2. *The scene:* a parent across the table from the teacher, deciding what comes next. Today:
   a report card and years of noticing held in memory. Wanted: "a clear, shareable
   [account] that brings school evidence and evidence from the rest of their child's life
   together," so that "when they're deciding what to build on, what support to offer, or what
   opportunities to explore, more of the child's story is there."
3. *Between meetings:* "They keep adding to it as life happens: something their child said or
   did, a photo, a teacher's comment, feedback from a coach, or something the child wants
   included. They don't have to turn every moment into an assessment." It should "connect new
   evidence with what's already there, show recurring patterns and changes, and leave room
   for things that don't fit the earlier picture." "They shouldn't have to wait for a school
   meeting to find it useful."
4. *Where she is in it:* "They're buying something I designed from my experience as a teacher,
   principal, and education innovator. My thinking is built into what it considers, how it
   connects evidence, and the conclusions it should and shouldn't draw. They're not buying
   ongoing one-to-one access to me." Access is Learn From the Room and the Inner Circle. The
   bio promises "the value of my work: helping parents preserve and use the evidence of their
   child's learning and growth beyond grades."
5. *Not to be mistaken for:* tutoring, therapy, a school platform, an app for kids, a grading
   tool. "It isn't there to rank or diagnose. It's parent-owned." "My broader work isn't
   tutoring, therapy, or teaching parents how to parent."

*Note on "dashboard" (answer 2):* the ratified record ships a report with a reading path and
a shareable PDF; visualizations are V3 and school-facing tools V5. Copy says "shareable" and
"brings school and home together"; it does not say "dashboard" until the product does.

| Element | Truer Measure |
| --- | --- |
| **Character** | A K–12 parent |
| **Wants** | To do something with what they already see in their child — and to have more than a grade to speak for them |
| **Problem — external** | The report card measures performance against a narrow list |
| **Problem — internal** | *I'm relying on memory to explain my child; the years of noticing were never brought together* |
| **Problem — philosophical** | A child's learning shouldn't be lost because the form had no room for it. In the founder's words: *if a report card only captures part of a child's learning, where does the rest of the evidence go?* |
| **Guide — empathy** | She sat across from parents looking for their child inside a paragraph of comments |
| **Guide — authority** | Teacher, principal, education innovator; her thinking is built into what the software considers, how it connects evidence, and the conclusions it should and shouldn't draw |
| **Plan** | 1. Get the guide → 2. Bring what you have — report cards, assessments, what you've noticed, what your child wants included → 3. Read the Hidden Report Card; keep adding as life happens; bring it to the table |
| **Call to action — direct** | Start at truermeasure.com |
| **Call to action — transitional** | Comment REPORT for the one-page guide |
| **Failure** | Each observation remembered briefly and then lost; the meeting decided on a report card and memory |
| **Success** | A fuller account of the child, parent-owned, on the table when what comes next is decided |
| **One-liner** | *Grades shouldn't be the only evidence that speaks for your child. The Hidden Report Card brings together what school records and what you see — kept across the years, parent-owned — so you have a fuller account of your child when it counts.* |

Test for any line of copy: if the founder is the subject of the first sentence, it's the
guide's story, not the hero's. Pins and bios open with the parent; the founder's story lands
on the parent within two sentences.

### 4.1 Hierarchy, in the order a stranger needs it

1. **The value statement.** *You notice things about your child that no school will ever
   see. Bring them here. We'll show you what they're evidence of — and how they connect to
   everything else, across the years.* This replaces "a report card was never designed to
   tell the whole story" as the top line. The report-card contrast stays; it moves down.
2. **Proof.** "This made me cry. It hit me right in the heart. I'll need to read it again."
   Three of three founding families came back with more, unasked. Neither is on the site.
   With permission (D-4), they lead the page after the hero.
3. **A glimpse of the artefact.** One anonymized, consented excerpt — a single section with
   its evidence tap — so a stranger sees the shape of what $597 buys. A real excerpt beats a
   mock; credibility is the whole brand.
4. **The founder.** Twenty-five years writing and signing the report cards. Already on the
   page and already right.
5. **The qualities grid.** The research-defended twelve. Already right; keep the notes file
   current.
6. **The moments.** The school-calendar list already on the site *plus* the home moments the
   founding families actually brought: the thing she did at the hairdresser, the snack made
   the way you like it. Both triggers belong; today only one is shown.

**What not to say.** "Unlock," "potential," "transform" — the button copy is the one place
the site currently sounds like everyone else. Nothing that claims diagnosis, prediction or
assessment. Nothing about AI as the hero; the founder's judgment is the hero and the engine
is how it scales.

## 5. Channels

### 5.1 The network — most of the thirty

Personal asks, by the founder, one at a time. Planning assumption, not a measurement:
**one in three to one in five warm asks converts** at the founding rate, which puts the ask
list at roughly 100–150 names for thirty families. Start with the parents who already have
a workaround — they are known to her by name.

The ask is not a pitch; it is the value statement in the founder's voice plus the founding
offer, with the shareable object attached so the person can pass it on without having to
explain the product themselves.

### 5.2 Word of mouth — the rest of the thirty, and the source of the clean ten

No rewards, no links, no machinery. One ask per founding family, made once, **after their
first return** — that is the moment they have something to say. "Is there one parent you'd
send this to?" The name goes on the founder's list. Those the founder does not know are
clean-cohort candidates and are contacted by the standard path, not by her phone.

Referral attribution: S03 stores how each family arrived, verbatim. The referral-sourced
percentage for this cohort is readable from it without any build.

**The content spine: the lens applied to things every parent already holds.**

- What a 200-character report-card comment can and cannot say.
- What "works well with others" is actually evidence of — and what it hides.
- The difference between a confident child and a compliant one, in the words teachers use.
- Why the strengths that recur across three years never appear on any single report card.
- How to read a report card the week it arrives (timed to §8).

Every piece is the product's method demonstrated on public material, at the standard in
the founder's own writing rules: research-backed, calibrated, no AI phrasing, no
overclaiming. A piece that sounds like the report is the proof.

### 5.4 The shareable object

One artefact the network can forward: **"How to read your child's report card comment"** —
a short, designed guide that applies the lens to the document every parent gets in
November. It is also a pretotype: a parent who saves it is showing the early-adopter
trait. Delivered through the existing waitlist-button infrastructure (GHL tag,
`website-compliance-remediation-spec.md` T7 governs the privacy notice on the form). No
drip sequence behind it — one delivery, one line saying the Hidden Report Card exists.

## 7. Funnel and measurement

**The funnel as it will exist.** Content or a personal ask → site → Stripe payment link →
webhook provisions the account and mints the registration link (F01, built) → welcome
email (GHL) → registration → upload (F02, built) → founder produces the first report by hand
→ founder review and release (F06, built) → the parent reads → the parent returns.

**Numbers to read weekly, in this order:**

| Signal | Source | Reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asks made / families paid | Founder's list | Whether the planning assumption in §5.1 holds |
| Arrival attribution split (network / referral / social / unknown) | S03 | The referral-sourced share, against ~30% early |
| Guide requests | GHL tag | Whether the shareable object travels |
| Upload within 7 days of registration | App | Whether entry lands people in the product |
| First report delivered within 5 business days | Founder's list | The promise, kept or not, per family |
| Recognition vs agreement at first read | F4 reflection capture (when built); until then the founder's note per family | The benefit signal |
| **Exchanges after the first report**, per family | App uploads and observations; the founder's inbox until the app carries them | **The continuation metric — the number the second year is priced on** |
| **Clean cohort: unprompted return within eight weeks** | Founder's list | **The experiment. The single most important cell in this table** |

**What "worked" means at the checkpoint (March 2027).** The four validation behaviours,
consistently present across the founding thirty, *and* a clean cohort in which a meaningful
share — more than one or two — returned unprompted. A founding cohort that returns and a
clean cohort that does not is a finding, not a failure: it says the product today is the
relationship, and V2's job is to move that into the software before scaling it.

## 10. Risks named

- **Manual production load.** Forty first reports by hand, over five months, while F03 is
  being built. The plan holds at 10/month only if nothing else claims that week. If the
  engine slips, the calendar slips with it — never the five-day promise.
- **Founding and clean pull against each other.** The founding offer promises access; the
  experiment requires distance. The only protection is D-6, kept.
- **Terms and refunds (compliance D5) block the stranger campaign.** Founding families can
  proceed on trust; a stranger cannot be sold an auto-renewing subscription without live
  terms. Sequence the clean cohort after `/terms` ships.
- **Two jurisdictions, one set of documents.** The legal drafts were written for a Canadian
  buyer. A Florida family is a US consumer buying an auto-renewing subscription about a
  child's data. Until counsel has reviewed for that, every Florida sale is exposure — and
  Florida is where the clean cohort comes from, so the experiment waits on it.
- **The French site does not exist,** and this cohort does not need it. The legal French still
  needs a human translator (compliance §6) before `justevaleur.ca` can ever ship.
- **Small social audience mistaken for a channel.** If the founder measures content by
  reach, it will look like failure. Measure it by what the network forwards.
- **Quoting children.** Proof must never name or identify a child. Parent quotes, first
  names or initials, consent in writing.
