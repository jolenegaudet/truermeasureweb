# Social strategy — the channel that has to sell

> **Status: DRAFT — 2026-09-05.** Companion to `marketing-strategy-founding-40.md`; replaces its
> §5.3–5.4 as the operating document for social. Written after an audit of the live accounts
> (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Spotify) on 2026-09-05, read from the founder's own
> logged-in dashboards.
>
> **The one-line finding.** The accounts are not small; they reach roughly 200,000 views a month.
> They are pointed at the wrong room, and the exit from that room to the site is 18 taps a
> month. Volume is not the problem. Sequence is.

### Podcast — *Drive to Work · Drive it Home* (Spotify)

### What the audit says, in five lines

1. **Reach is not the constraint.** ~200k views a month across two platforms is a real
   audience. Eighteen link taps is the constraint. Attention is not being converted into
   visits, let alone conversations.
2. **The room is peers, not buyers.** Fifty-nine percent of the Facebook audience is 45 or
   older; the cities are the founder's own Acadian New Brunswick; the birthday comments are
   from former colleagues. Parents of school-age children are a minority in the room, and
   Florida is absent.
3. **The feed has been trained on Jolene-the-person.** Reinvention, outfits, travel, gifts win
   on Instagram; they are honest and they are hers — and they teach the algorithm to serve her
   to people who like her, not to parents who need the product. Product posts lose by 5–10×.
4. **Every product CTA is a cliff.** Post → truermeasure.com → US$597 auto-renewing. No proof
   step, no conversation, no smaller yes. Nobody buys a year of anything about their child from
   a caption.
5. **The format that works is already hers.** On Facebook the three best-travelling pieces of
   original content are branded text cards carrying one sharp observation about school and
   children. Forty-six percent of their views are strangers. The 3 September card is the value
   statement phrased as a question.

One correction to make now, independent of strategy: the 25 August caption says *"Tout est
aussi offert en français."* Nothing is offered in French today — no site, no report, no legal
documents. For a brand whose entire proposition is being right, that line has to go.

## 2. What "sells" means for a US$597 subscription

A post does not close this sale. A post produces one of two handoffs, and the strategy is
judged on how many of each it produces per week:

- **Handoff A — the site**, after the M1–M8 changes: proof, the founder note, the excerpt, the
  offer stated as a year of exchanges.
- **Handoff B — a conversation**: a DM that mentions a child, a reply to the guide, a fifteen-
  minute call. High-ticket trust purchases close here, and the founder has the capacity for it
  (10+ families a month, by her own estimate).

Everything below is arranged to move a stranger from the feed to A or B in the fewest steps,
with one smaller yes in between.

## 3. The four changes

### 3.1 One buyer, one job, in every post

Every piece of content is for **the parent of a K–12 child who already writes
things down about her child**, and every piece applies the lens to something she already
holds: the report-card comment, the note home, "works well with others," the child who was
excited for school in September and isn't in November.

What leaves the feed for this campaign:

- **The three-tier pitch.** Community and Proximity are waitlists for V6 and V7. On social they
  dilute the one thing being sold and they talk about the founder's ecosystem rather than the
  parent's child. The 25 August post is the pattern to retire.
- **Educator-facing content** (*Drive to Work*, "what are we fitting into the school day"). It
  is good, it builds the peer audience, and the peer audience does not buy. Park it until
  February, or move it to LinkedIn where those people are.

What stays, deliberately rationed: the founder's own story — one in five posts, always landing
on the parent ("I signed report cards for 25 years; here is what they never said about your
kid"). Lifestyle stays in Stories, where it keeps the account human without training the feed.

### 3.2 The format spine

Facebook has already run the experiment. The lead format is the **observation card**: one
sentence, the founder's name and the domain, no face, no hashtag wall. It is the product's
method — a sharp, research-grounded observation about a child — demonstrated in public, and
it travels to strangers.

Weekly spine, replacing three-a-day with one strong piece a day plus Stories:

| Day | Piece | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Observation card | Travel — the thing a grandmother forwards to a parent |
| Tue | Face-to-camera reel, 45–60s | Trust — the founder saying the card out loud, with a principal's-office story behind it |
| Wed | Observation card | Travel |
| Thu | **Proof or artefact** | Sell — a founding-family quote (D-4), an anonymized excerpt, a "three of three came back" fact, the honest "here's what a Hidden Report Card looks like" |
| Fri | Card phrased as a question to parents | Conversation — the ask in the caption is the keyword (§3.3), not the URL |
| Sat/Sun | Stories only; one personal post if wanted | Human |

Same piece on Facebook and Instagram, sized for each; Facebook feed first, since 65% of its
traffic comes from there. Reels for Tuesday only — they are 41% of Instagram views but the
cheapest format to get wrong.

Made in one batch a week: five cards and two reels in an afternoon. Twenty minutes a day for
replies — every comment, every DM. The dashboard's own prompt ("reply to 10 comments, 3/10")
is the algorithm telling you the same thing.

### 3.3 Replace the cliff with a ladder

The URL leaves the caption. The ladder is:

1. **Post** — card or reel, one observation.
2. **Keyword** — "Comment or DM **BEYOND** and I'll send you the one-page guide to reading your
   child's report card comment." The smaller yes.
3. **The guide** — the shareable object from the marketing strategy, delivered by DM and email.
   One follow-up line, once: *the Hidden Report Card exists; here is what it is.* No drip.
   (Compliance T7 governs the privacy notice on the capture.)
4. **The conversation** — anyone who replies to the guide with something about their child
   gets an offer of fifteen minutes with the founder. That is Handoff B.
5. **The site**, for those who go straight there — link in bio to the homepage after M1–M8,
   never to the Stripe checkout.

Delivery of step 3 is automatable — GoHighLevel already holds the contacts and the waitlist
tags, and DM-keyword tools (ManyChat and equivalents) do the Instagram/Facebook half. Which
tool is a founder decision (D-S3); the ladder does not depend on it, only the labour does.

### 3.4 Route through the peers instead of fighting them

The 45-and-over New Brunswick audience is not the buyer. It is the buyer's mother, aunt,
neighbour and former teacher. Treat it as the distribution layer it already is: Monday and
Wednesday cards carry a line like *"Know a parent with a kid in school, K–12? Send them this."*
The word-of-mouth channel in the marketing strategy runs partly through this audience, and
it costs nothing to say so out loud.

## 4. Florida

The audit is unambiguous: **organic social does not reach South Florida today.** Zero Florida
cities in the audience, 12% US overall. The LinkedIn headline says the founder is *based in
NB and South Florida* — she is physically there part of the year, which is an asset the
accounts do not yet reflect. Three ways in:

- **The collaborator's network.** The American collaborator shares the guide and the Thursday
  proof pieces into their own channels. This is the primary path and the one consistent with
  the clean cohort — those parents arrive without the founder's phone number.
- **The founder's own Florida presence.** Local parent groups, the school the household is
  attached to, in-person conversations while she is there. These parents *will* know her, so
  they belong to the founding cohort, not the clean one — but they are Florida buyers, and
  they seed the clean cohort by referral. Tag Florida-made content with Florida (location,
  school-calendar language: "Q1 report cards") so the platforms learn there is an audience
  there.
- **A small paid test — proposal, founder's call (D-S1).** Boost the two best observation
  cards, not the product post, to women 30–45 with school-age children in Miami-Dade, Broward
  and Palm Beach, sending to the guide, not the checkout. Cap: **CA$150 across the November
  window**, and stop if the guide costs more than CA$5 per request. The draft boost sitting in
  the ad tools (CA$55, audience named in the founder's vocabulary rather than a parent's,
  pointing at a product post) is the wrong shape for this and should be discarded.

Paid acquisition sits outside the ratified "no referral machinery, no nudges" rules — those
govern how existing families are treated, not how strangers first hear of the product. It is
still a departure from how the founder has grown, which is why it is a decision and not a
default. No Florida spend before the US legal review (marketing strategy §3) clears a Florida
buyer.

## 5. The podcast

Season 2 opens in the fall. For this campaign it is **parent episodes only** — *Drive it
Home* — timed to the two report-card windows: the first week of November (Florida Q1) and the
last week of November (New Brunswick). Each episode is cut into one Tuesday reel and one
observation card; the episode description carries the keyword, not the URL. *Drive to Work*
resumes in February. This is a founder decision (D-S2); the reasoning is the same as §3.1 —
one buyer for one campaign.

## 6. What to measure, weekly

| Signal | Today | Reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Link taps (IG) + link clicks (FB) | 18 / month | Whether the ladder's bottom rung gets traffic at all |
| Guide requests (keyword + form) | 0 | The smaller yes — the number the whole channel is judged on |
| DMs that mention a child | unknown | Handoff B forming |
| Conversations held | 0 | The sales pipeline |
| Sales with attribution "social" (S03) | 0 | The channel's share of the forty |
| Non-follower share of views | IG 38% / FB 46% | Whether cards keep travelling |
| Comment reply rate | 3/10 | Whether the founder is in the conversation |

**Planning assumptions, not measurements**, so the arithmetic is visible: ten clean-cohort
families from social over four months is 2–3 a month. If one conversation in three becomes a
family, that is 8 conversations a month; if one guide in ten becomes a conversation, 80 guide
requests a month; if one tap in three becomes a guide request, 240 taps a month against 18
today. **That is a thirteen-fold change in the bottom of the funnel.** It is not a reason to
lower the target; it is the honest statement of what "a channel that sells" has to do, and why
the Florida collaborator matters more than any boost.

## 9. Risks named

- **Cutting volume feels like losing.** Three-a-day to one-a-day will drop raw views for a
  few weeks. The metric that matters is guide requests, not views; hold the nerve for a month.
- **The peer audience may resent being sold to.** They are being asked to forward, not to buy;
  the copy has to say so plainly.
- **The founder's face is the trust asset and the distraction.** Tuesday reels keep the face;
  the feed stops being about the face.
- **Florida without the collaborator is a paid channel.** If the collaborator's network does
  not materialise, the clean cohort depends entirely on D-S1.
- **Nothing in French.** The captions claim it; the product doesn't have it. Until the French
  site exists, French-language posts should say what is true: the founder speaks French, the
  product is in English.
