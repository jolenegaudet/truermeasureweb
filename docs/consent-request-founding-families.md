## 1. What is being asked, exactly

Two separate permissions. A family can say yes to one and no to the other.

**A. To quote them.** Their own words about the Hidden Report Card — the ones they already
sent — used on truermeasure.com and in social posts, attributed by **first name or initial
only**, no town, no school, no child's name, no child's age. Example of what the quote would
look like in use: *"This made me cry. I'll need to read it again." — M., New Brunswick*
(and "New Brunswick" only if they're comfortable with the province).

**B. To show one section of their child's report.** One passage — a few paragraphs — from the
Hidden Report Card itself, with its evidence panel, so a stranger can see the shape of what
they'd receive. Before it's shown, everything identifying is removed by hand: the child's name
and pronouns replaced, the school and grade removed, teachers' names removed, and **any
detail specific enough to recognise in a small town taken out or reworded**. They see the
anonymized version and approve *that*, not the original. Only one family needs to say yes to
B.

Both permissions are:

- **Revocable.** They can withdraw any time; it comes down from the site and no new post uses
  it. Already-printed or already-posted material can't be recalled — say so plainly.
- **Specific.** Yes to the words they've already written, not to anything they say later.
  Anything new gets asked again.
- **Recorded.** In writing — a text or email reply saying yes is enough. Kept in the ledger.

For an older child who has read their own report: ask the child too, in the parent's presence.
It's their story.

## 5. Preparing the excerpt (only after one "yes to B")

1. Pick a section whose *pattern* is generic — curiosity, persistence, how the child affects
   other people — not one built on a single memorable anecdote.
2. Replace the child's name with a neutral one or "your child"; fix pronouns consistently.
3. Remove school, grade, year, teacher names, subject names that would narrow it, and any
   detail a neighbour would recognise (a sport, a club, a specific incident).
4. Keep the evidence panel, but the evidence lines get the same treatment — teacher comments
   quoted only where the wording is generic.
5. Read it as if you lived on their street. If you'd know who it was, cut more.
6. Send the family the exact image that will go on the site. Their "yes" is to that image.
