# The marketing plan — north star

*One job: make the target parent understand the three key features of the Hidden Report Card — and pay. Everything published, posted, or said in a DM does one of the three things below, or it doesn't go out. We talk only to parents who match the profile. Only a payment counts as proof. Ten by 30 November. 2026-09-08.*

---

## Who we talk to — and nobody else

A parent of a child in grades 1–6 who already knows the report card is not the whole story. They keep notes, photos, a strategy book — something the school has never seen. They have at least two years of report cards and a report card or a meeting coming in the next ninety days. They can pay US$597.

**The screen, before any conversation goes past hello:**
1. *What grade is your child in?* — 1 to 6.
2. *Do you keep notes about your child that school has never seen — anything, anywhere?* — yes.
3. *Is there a report card or a meeting coming up in the next couple of months?* — yes.

Two of three: continue. Otherwise: thank them, send them the series, no offer, no fifteen minutes. Every conversation is logged with the three answers. Time spent with anyone who doesn't match is time taken from the ten.

## The one sentence they should be able to repeat

> **The Hidden Report Card is one place for everything about my child's learning — school and home — that reads it all across the years and keeps going as my child grows.**

If a parent can say that back in their own words, the marketing worked. Every piece below exists to get them to one of its three clauses.

| | |

## The order we say it

**Read across the years first.** It is the reason to care and the thing nobody else does. **One place** second — it is how the read becomes possible, and it is the feature a parent can act on tonight. **Keeps going** third — it is why this is a year, not a document, and it is what makes the price make sense.

The series (Parts 1–6) sets up all three without naming the product: it is the argument that the report card is incomplete. The pain weeks that follow are one feature each, in rotation: week 2 → one place · week 3 → one place (the record with dates) · week 4 → read across the years (the verb) · week 5 → read across the years (the rest of the child) · week 6 → one place (the quiet child's record) · week 7 → keeps going.

## Proof — only money counts

**Ten paying, profile-matched families by 30 November 2026.** A Stripe payment from a parent who passed the screen. Nothing else is proof: not a save, not a share, not a DM, not "this made me cry," not a promise to sign up after the report card. Those are things to listen for, not things to count.

Checked every Monday, four numbers: **conversations started · matched · offered · paid.**

- Matched but not offered → the fifteen-minute read isn't happening fast enough.
- Offered but not paid → the pitch or the price.
- Started but not matched → looking in the wrong place.

**What to listen for along the way** (leading, not proof): a stranger sends one thing about their child (feature 1 landed) · a stranger sends a report-card line or asks *"what would it say about mine?"* (feature 2) · a parent asks what happens at the next report card (feature 3) · a parent says the one sentence back in their own words (all three).

Short of ten on 30 November: the message or the customer changes. The features don't.

## What is not in this plan

The Room and the Circle (waitlists only until ten). French. Research and citations as selling points. The founder's story as the lead (it lands on the parent within two sentences, or it's cut). Anything a parent can't repeat.
