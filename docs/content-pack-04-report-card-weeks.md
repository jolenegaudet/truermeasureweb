## 1. Florida Q1 — six pieces around Day 0

Florida elementary and middle schools run nine-week quarters. Q1 report cards go home in
early November, letter grades and a conduct/effort mark, with parent–teacher conferences in
the same fortnight. Location-tag every post (Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton, alternating).

**Day −2 — card F1 — before it comes home**

> Before the report card comes home this week,
> write down three things your kid did this quarter
> that no teacher saw.
>
> You'll want them on Thursday.

Caption: *Q1 report cards are days away. The grade will take your attention first. Get the rest of the story down before it does.*

**Day 0 — card F2 — it's here**

> The first thing you looked at was the grade.
>
> The second thing should be the verb.
>
> "Is beginning to." "Continues to." "Needs to."
> Motion, motion, next step. None of them is a verdict.

Caption: *Report cards came home today in [district]. Read the comment twice: once for what it says, once for what it can't.*

**Day +1 — reel F3 — "The report card came home. Here's how I'd read it."**

> Your kid's Q1 report card came home yesterday. Here's how I'd read it, as someone who signed thousands of them.
>
> One. The letter is a measure of the room's target. An A means the target was met. It doesn't say whether the target was worth reaching, or whether it was reached with effort or with boredom.
>
> Two. The comment was written by an adult who has known your child for nine weeks. It's a first read. First reads are honest and incomplete.
>
> Three. Find the one line that's specific — a moment, a *when*. That's the teacher spending scarce characters on your kid. Underline it. Ask about it at the conference.
>
> Four. Now look at what isn't there. The thing she built at home. The question he keeps asking. That isn't a gap in your kid. It's a gap in the form.
>
> That's the part I built a place for. DM me "BEYOND" to start. 

Caption: *A letter grade measures the room's target. The rest of your kid is somewhere else. DM "BEYOND."*

**Day +2 — card F4 — the conference**

> Fifteen minutes with the teacher next week.
>
> Walk in with a page, not a memory.
> Three things you noticed. One question that wants
> an example, not a rating.

Caption: *"Can you give me an example of a time you saw that?" turns a rating into a moment. Moments are evidence. Ratings are summaries of evidence you haven't seen.*

**Day +3 — Thursday product — "Put it in"**

Card:

> The report card goes in the drawer.
>
> Or it goes in.

Caption:

Add what the teacher said at the conference. The account connects the new to what's already there: what's recurring, what's changed, what doesn't fit the earlier picture yet.
>
> By Q3 you're not reading a report card. You're reading a year.
>
> Parent-owned. Start at the link in bio, or DM me "BEYOND."

**Day +4 — Friday card F5 — question + keyword**

> What did the Q1 report card get wrong
> about your kid?

Caption: *Not the grade — the description. Tell me the line that isn't your child. And DM "BEYOND" if you want to know what I'd look for instead.*

## 2. New Brunswick — six pieces around Day 0

New Brunswick's first report card of the year goes home in late November, anglophone and
francophone districts alike, with the first formal parent–teacher meeting close behind. The
founder's audience here is the peer network — grandmothers, aunts, former colleagues — so
every card carries the forwarding line. Location-tag Moncton or Dieppe. One card in French,
posted as its own piece, not a translation stacked under the English.

**Day −2 — card N1 — before it comes home**

> Le bulletin arrive cette semaine.
>
> Avant de l'ouvrir, écris trois choses que ton enfant
> a faites cet automne — que personne à l'école n'a vues.

Caption: *Tu vas en avoir besoin à la rencontre. Si tu connais un parent d'un enfant à l'école, envoie-lui ceci.*

**Day 0 — card N2 — it's here**

> The first report card of the year
> was written by someone who has known your child
> for eleven weeks.
>
> It's a first read. Treat it like one.

Caption: *Honest, and incomplete. That's not a criticism of the teacher; it's what eleven weeks can hold. Know a parent with a kid in school? Send them this one.*

**Day +1 — reel N3 — "What I'd tell every parent this week"**

> First report cards went home in New Brunswick this week. Here's what I'd tell every parent, having signed them for twenty years.
>
> The comment box has room for about two sentences. The teacher chose those two sentences out of everything they saw. So the most useful question at the meeting isn't "is this right?" It's "what made you write *that*?"
>
> The code beside the comment — whatever your district calls it — is a position on a scale the school set. It tells you where your kid landed on the school's list. It can't tell you what your kid is on nobody's list.
>
> And the thing you noticed in October — the one you meant to write down and didn't — is exactly the kind of evidence that never makes it onto the page. Write it down tonight. That's the record only you can keep.
>
> DM me "BEYOND" to start. 

Caption: *Two sentences and a code. The rest of your kid is yours to keep. DM "BEYOND." — Si tu connais un parent, envoie-lui ceci.*

**Day +2 — card N4 — the meeting**

> At the meeting, ask one question
> the report card can't answer:

> turns out to be harder than she expected?"

Caption: *The single most revealing thing a classroom sees that a form can't hold. Pass it on to a parent who has a meeting this week.*

## 3. On location in South Florida

The founder is there for both windows. Three things that only work in person, none of which
need a script:

- **Stories, daily, in the Florida week:** the report card envelope, the conference sign-up
  sheet, the car-line — "if your kid's Q1 report card came home today, DM me BEYOND." Location
  tag on every one.
- **One live, the evening of Day 0:** "Read your kid's Q1 report card with me." Fifteen
  minutes, no slides. Parents bring the comment; she reads it out loud the way she would as a
  principal. The live is the Handoff B in public.
- **In-person asks:** the parents she meets there are the founding cohort, not the clean one
  (marketing strategy §2). Every one of them is a referral seed for the strangers.
