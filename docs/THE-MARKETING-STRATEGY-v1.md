# The marketing strategy

*The one document. Everything else in this folder is working material behind it. If a piece of content, a DM, or a page can't be traced to a line here, it doesn't go out. Ratified by the founder 2026-09-08.*

---

## The customer

A parent of a child in grades 1–6 who already knows the report card isn't the whole story, keeps some kind of notes about their child, and has a report card or a meeting coming in the next ninety days.

We talk only to this parent. Three questions before any conversation goes past hello — *what grade is your child in? · do you keep notes about your child that school has never seen? · is there a report card or a meeting coming up?* Two of three, continue. Otherwise, thank them and send the series. No offer.

## Their problem, in their words

> "It doesn't match up with my kid."
> "I was blindsided at the conference."
> "When something is written down, it counts."

Underneath all three: **the school has a written account of my child and I don't — and when it matters, I have nothing in my hands but my memory.**

## What they must understand to buy

The three features, said as what each one does for *their* child — never as features.

**1. Your child gets one record that includes what you see.**
Not just the school's paper — the coach's comment, the thing she said in the car, the assessment, the note home. In one place, dated, yours.
→ *Nothing about your child gets lost or lives only in your memory.*

**2. Your child gets read — across the years, by someone who signed report cards for twenty years — and every sentence is backed by something you gave.**
What keeps showing up. What the report card doesn't realize it's telling you. Where your picture and the school's disagree, and what that means.
→ *You stop wondering whether you're imagining it. You have the account, and it holds.*

**3. Your child's account keeps growing.**
The next report card goes in, and you get *what's changed* — not another document. Six updates a year included.
→ *You walk into every meeting from here on with more of your child than a report card and a memory.*

The sentence a parent should be able to say back: **one place for everything about my child's learning — school and home — read across the years, and it keeps going as my child grows.**

## Why they'll believe it

Not claims. A demonstration on their own child.

- **In the DM, or the fifteen minutes:** the parent brings one report card and one thing they wrote down. The founder reads them side by side, live — what the comment can hold, what it can't, what the parent's note is evidence of, the question to ask the teacher. That is feature 2 happening to their child before they pay.
- **In public, every week:** the same read on report-card lines parents send in. Never a name.
- **Then the proof:** three founding families; three came back with more, unasked — an assessment, a strengths inventory written on a parent's own initiative, a family sorting their children's qualities together. Their words, once they consent.

## How they meet it

1. **The founder's series first** — *There's More to Your Child Than School Can Measure*, six parts on six days. It is the argument that the report card is incomplete, without naming the product.
2. **Then one problem a week, in parents' words** — it doesn't match my kid · blindsided at the meeting · "working towards" · nothing positive in the boxes · nobody needs to talk about my kid · keep looking. Each week: the problem, a tool a parent can use tonight, the public read, the product for that parent, the question.
3. **Every piece ends at the same door:** *DM "BEYOND" to start.* Behind it: the three questions, the read on their kid, the offer.
4. **The founder's own asks and the collaborator's network** feed the same door directly — New Brunswick and South Florida.

## When they buy

The moment the pain is live: the report card in hand, the meeting on the calendar. Early November in South Florida. Late November in New Brunswick. Everything before that builds the understanding; that fortnight is the sale.

## The offer

**US$597 a year, per child.** Founding Families: the first 40, 40% off the first year with FOUNDING40. The first Hidden Report Card is reviewed by the founder before release. Six updates a year included; extra updates US$10. Renews yearly until cancelled.

Learn from the Room and the Inner Circle are not sold until the tenth family has paid.

## Proof it worked

**Ten paying, profile-matched parents by 30 November 2026.** Only money. A Stripe payment from a parent who passed the screen. Nothing else counts — not a save, a share, a DM, or "this made me cry." Those are things to listen for.

Four numbers every Monday: **started · matched · offered · paid.**
Matched but not offered — the read isn't happening fast enough. Offered but not paid — the pitch or the price. Started but not matched — looking in the wrong place.

At ten: widen. Short of ten on 30 November: change the customer or the problem. Not the features.
