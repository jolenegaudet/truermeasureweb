# The Hidden Report Card — Go-to-Market Strategy

**Truer Measure · Founding cohort · 8 September – 30 November 2026**
*The one document. Version 2, 2026-09-08. Version 1 is kept beside it as `THE-MARKETING-STRATEGY-v1.md`.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

Truer Measure sells the Hidden Report Card: parent-owned software that keeps everything about a child's learning — the school's record and the parent's — in one place, reads it across the years into a written account, and updates as the child grows. Price: US$597 a year per child; Founding Families 40% off the first year.

The goal of this plan is **ten paying, profile-matched families by 30 November 2026.** Only a payment counts. The plan reaches that number by concentrating on one customer, one problem, and one buying window — the report-card fortnight in November — and by selling through a demonstration on the parent's own child rather than through description.

The strategic bet: **parents already hold the evidence about their child; what they lack is standing.** The school has a written account and they have a memory. Everything below exists to make that gap felt, then closed, for one parent at a time.

## 2. The insight

Across 19 public threads (r/Parenting, r/ADHDparenting, Mumsnet, CBC) parents describe the same moment in the same words: *"It doesn't match up with my kid."* *"I was blindsided at the conference."* *"I know I'm not delusional on this."* *"When something is written down, it counts."* The 1,900-upvote thread is a mother crying at a conference because a teacher told her who her daughter is when she isn't there.

Three things are true at once:

1. **The parent sees more of the child than the school does** — and knows it.
2. **The school's account is the only one in writing** — so it wins every conversation, every decision, every meeting.
3. **The school's account is incomplete by design.** Report cards were built (1848–1920s) for reliability and sorting, not description. Everything a parent wants to know about their child is variance; the form was built to remove it.

The product does not compete with the report card. It gives the parent's account the same standing — written, dated, read across years — and then reads both together. *The second column, finally beside the first.*

## 3. Positioning

**For** parents of a child in grades 1–6 who already know the report card isn't the whole story and have started writing things down,
**the Hidden Report Card is** the only account of a child that keeps the school's record and the parent's together and reads them across the years,
**so that** the parent walks into every meeting and decision with more than a report card and a memory.

**Unlike**
- **the report card** — one room, one list, two hundred characters, built for consistency;
- **the notes app / the ChatGPT strategy book** parents already keep — no memory across years, no connection to the school's record, no standing;
- **the psychoeducational assessment** — one moment, clinical frame, US$2,000–4,000, tells you what's wrong;
- **a tutor or educational consultant** — buys hours, not a record; ends when the hours do;

**the Hidden Report Card** is parent-owned, evidence-traced, cumulative, and reads the child rather than grading or diagnosing.

**Category we claim:** *a living account of a child's education — school and beyond.* We do not say "dashboard," "portfolio," "assessment," or "report" alone.

## 4. Customer

### 4.1 The target profile
| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Child** | Grades 1–6 · at least two years of report cards |
| **Parent** | Already keeps notes, photos, a folder, a strategy book — anything the school has never seen |
| **Trigger** | A report card or a meeting in the next ninety days |
| **Means** | Can pay US$597 without an internal negotiation |
| **Where** | New Brunswick (founder's network, its forwarding layer) · South Florida (collaborator's network; founder present through November) |

### 4.2 The screen — we talk to nobody else
1. *What grade is your child in?* — 1 to 6.
2. *Do you keep notes about your child that school has never seen?* — yes.
3. *Is there a report card or a meeting coming in the next couple of months?* — yes.

Two of three: continue. Otherwise thank them, send the series, no offer. Every conversation logged with the three answers so the profile can be corrected by evidence.

### 4.3 Why this slice
The market is K–12 (ratified). The first ten come from the slice where the pain is sharpest and the demonstration cleanest: young enough that the parent still holds most of the record, old enough to have two years of it. Widening comes after the tenth payment.

### 4.4 Reachable population, honestly
There is no bought list. The reachable population is: the founder's New Brunswick network (educators and parents she has known for twenty years — the Facebook audience is 1,900 people, largely 45+, largely peers, who *know* the parents); the collaborator's South Florida network; and strangers who arrive through the content. The plan does not assume scale. It assumes roughly **140 conversations** (§8) can be opened in twelve weeks by one founder working the door daily.

## 5. Messaging architecture

**The spine** (every reel opens here): *If you've watched a brilliant kid start to disappear in a system built for a different era — this is for you.*

**The one sentence a parent should be able to repeat:** *One place for everything about my child's learning — school and home — read across the years, and it keeps going as my child grows.*

**Three pillars — the features, said as what they do for her child:**

| Pillar | What the parent hears | The pain it answers | Proof point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. One record that includes what you see** | Every report card, note, assessment — and the coach's comment, the thing she said in the car, the photo. Dated. Yours. *Nothing about your child lives only in your memory.* | *"When something is written down, it counts."* · *"I was blindsided."* | Three founding families brought home material unprompted — an assessment, a strengths inventory, a family's qualities sort |
| **2. Your child gets read — and it holds** | A written account, across the years, by someone who signed report cards for twenty years. What keeps showing up. What the report card doesn't realize it's telling you. Every sentence traces to something you gave. Not a grade, not a diagnosis. *You stop wondering whether you're imagining it.* | *"It doesn't match up with my kid."* · *"Nothing positive in any box."* | The delivered report: six parts, nine pages, founder-reviewed. Parent quotes on consent |
| **3. It keeps going** | Add the next report card, ask for *What's Changed.* Six updates a year. *You walk into every meeting from here on with more than a report card and a memory.* | *"It would have been helpful to know much earlier."* · *"One term is a point."* | Three of three families returned with more, unasked |

**Objections, and the answer we give** (in the founder's voice — declarative, no defence):

| Objection | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| *"It's expensive."* | It is an investment. Most families bring a report card and a memory to the table. This is the whole story, in hand. |
| *"I already keep notes."* | Good — most families don't. Where were they at the last meeting? |
| *"The school won't care what I bring."* | The school responds to what's written down and dated. That's what this is. |
| *"Is this a diagnosis? A score?"* | Neither. It reads what has already happened, and every sentence shows its source. A psychologist's report enters as that professional's words, never as ours. |
| *"I need to talk to my partner."* | Of course. I can send something the two of you can read, or we can talk together for fifteen minutes. |
| *"Not the right time."* | The report card comes home in [weeks]. That's the moment I built this for. |

**Words we don't use:** unlock, potential, transform, dashboard, unlimited, "I'll tell you what I'd look for," any promise about outcomes for the child.

## 6. The offer and the price

**The Hidden Report Card — US$597 a year, per child.** Founding Families: the first 40, 40% off the first year with FOUNDING40; the founding rate ends when they're full. The first report is founder-reviewed. Six updates a year included; extra updates US$10. Renews yearly until cancelled.

Rationale: the price sits above a year of a notes app and far below an assessment or a tutor; it is priced as a year of standing, not as a document. It is public and on a Stripe checkout because it is software. **Learn from the Room and the Inner Circle are not sold until the tenth family has paid** — they stay as waitlists. They are the generalist's escape hatch, and the plan closes it.

Prerequisite before the first stranger pays: the refund position (compliance D5) decided and on the terms page; the US$10 update product created in Stripe.

## 7. How we sell — the demonstration

Nobody pays US$597 from a caption. A stranger arrives at one door — *DM "BEYOND" to start* — and behind it:

1. **The screen** (§4.2), in the founder's voice.
2. **The read** — fifteen minutes, or a DM exchange: the parent brings one report card and one thing they wrote down; the founder reads them side by side — what the comment can hold, what it can't, what the parent's note is evidence of, the question to ask the teacher. *That is pillar 2 happening to their child before they pay.*
3. **The offer** — the price, the founding rate, the invitation to start.
4. **Follow-up** — days 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13; the last message closes the file honestly.

In public, the same demonstration runs weekly as **the Read**: three real report-card lines, read on camera, never a name. It manufactures both proof and the DMs that feed the door.

## 8. The funnel — what ten actually requires

Planning assumptions, stated so they can be wrong in a visible way. Revise weekly against actuals.

| Stage | Assumption | Number needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Paid** | — | **10** |
| **Offered → paid** | 30% (a warm, demonstrated, founding-rate offer) | **34 offers** |
| **Matched → offered** | 60% (some go quiet before the read) | **56 matched** |
| **Started → matched** | 40% (peers, educators, wrong grade, no trigger) | **140 conversations started** |

Twelve weeks → **~12 conversations opened a week**, from:

| Source | Share of the 140 | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder's direct asks (New Brunswick) | ~60 | Highest match rate; she knows who keeps notes |
| Referrals from the NB peer audience | ~30 | Grandmothers, aunts, former colleagues forward to the parent |
| South Florida — collaborator + in person | ~25 | The stranger test inside the ten |
| Social → BEYOND DMs | ~25 | The Read and the pain weeks; matched share will be lowest here |

If the started→matched rate is under 30% after two weeks, the content is reaching the wrong room (today's audience is 59% aged 45+) and the founder's direct asks carry more of the load. If offered→paid is under 20%, the read isn't happening before the offer, or the price is being stated cold.

## 12. Measurement and kill criteria

**Every Monday, four numbers:** started · matched · offered · paid — against §8. Plus: BEYOND DMs from matched profiles (the content's only job), and the three screen answers per conversation.

**Only a payment is proof.** Saves, shares, "this made me cry," promises to sign up after the report card — listened to, not counted.

**At ten:** widen — the decoding problem, the second ten, then the Room.
**Short of ten on 30 November:** change the customer or the problem. Not the features. The features are what the founding families returned for; the question being tested is who else wants that and what they call it.

## 13. Risks

| Risk | Signal | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The room is peers, not parents (59% aged 45+, all NB) | Started→matched under 30% | Shift weight to direct asks; use the peer audience as forwarders, not buyers |
| The founder's time — 10 reports a month by hand while selling | Reports slip past five business days | Sell slower before promising faster; the promise holds |
| Florida sale before US legal review | Any Florida payment | No Florida offer until terms carry a US buyer |
| The founding offer and the clean-cohort test pull against each other | Florida families end up on the founder's phone | Collaborator holds the Florida relationships for eight weeks |
| Proof waits on consent (D-4) | Thursdays with no quote | Aggregate proof only until consent; the Read manufactures proof meanwhile |
| Drift back to many options | New offers, new features, new decks | This document; the "not doing" list |

## 14. What is deliberately not happening until the tenth payment

Room and Circle sales · French · a dashboard or visualization · new features · a referral programme · paid ads beyond the capped test · educator content as a channel · widening past grades 1–6 · a second strategy.
