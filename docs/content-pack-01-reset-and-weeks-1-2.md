## 3. Tuesday reel scripts

Face to camera, phone at eye level, no music under the voice, captions on. Forty-five to
sixty seconds is roughly 120–150 words spoken. The first sentence is the hook; it has to land
before a thumb moves. No URL in the reel or the caption — the keyword does the work.

### Script A — Tue 8 Sep — "What I was never allowed to write"

> I signed report cards for more than twenty years. First as a teacher writing them, then as a principal signing them.
>
> Here's what the comment box never had room for.
>
> The kid who organized the whole class when the substitute lost the plot. The one who asked the question that made me rethink the lesson. The one who was quiet all year and then, in June, said one thing that told me she'd been listening to everything.
>
> None of that has a line on the form. The form has about two hundred characters and a code.
>
> So parents sat across from me looking for their child inside a paragraph. And I watched them not find her.
>
> The things you notice at home aren't extra. They're the record the school can't keep.
>
> If you want the one-page guide to reading the comment you'll get in November — what it can say, what it can't — comment REPORT. I'll send it.

Caption: *More than twenty years of report cards. The comment box was never the problem. Its size was.*
*Comment REPORT for the guide.*

### Script B — Tue 15 Sep — "Confident or compliant"

> Your kid's report card says she's doing great. Here's the one question it can't answer.
>
> Is she confident, or is she compliant?
>
> On a report card they look identical. Same grade. Same "meets expectations." Same "a pleasure to have in class."
>
> The difference doesn't show up at school. It shows up at your kitchen table.
>
> Three things to notice this week. One: does she start things nobody asked for? Two: when something's hard, does she push, or does she wait to be told what to do? Three: when she's finished, does she want to show you — or is she just relieved it's over?
>
> None of that is on the form. All of it matters more than the form.
>
> Write it down. I mean it — one line, tonight. That's the record.
>
> Comment REPORT and I'll send you the guide to what the report card will and won't tell you in November.

Caption: *"Doing great" is a grade. Confident is a child. Comment REPORT for the guide.*

## 4. The keyword ladder — every line, in order

The ladder is: post → keyword → guide → conversation. These are the exact messages. Until a
DM tool is chosen (D-S3), the founder or the EA sends them by hand; at current volume that is
minutes a day.

**4.1 The ask (goes at the end of Friday captions and Tuesday reels)**

> Comment REPORT — or send it to me in a message — and I'll send you the one-page guide: how to read your child's report card comment. What it can say, what it can't, and what to write down before the meeting.

**4.2 The delivery (DM, within the hour if by hand)**

> Here it is: [guide link]
>
> One page. Read it before the report card lands, and keep it for the meeting.
>
> If reading it makes you think of something about your child that school has never measured — tell me. I read every one.
>
> — Jolene

*Until the guide exists, the delivery message is the same with one honest change: "It's
coming out on [date] — you're on the list, and you'll have it first." No drip behind it.*

**4.3 The one follow-up (48 hours later, once, never again)**

> The guide is the free version of what I do.
>
> The full version is the Hidden Report Card: your child's whole school record, every year and every teacher, read for what it's actually evidence of — and connected to what you see at home. Then it keeps going as you add what you notice.
>
> It's at truermeasure.com. If you'd rather talk first, I'll take fifteen minutes with you and your child's last report card and show you what I'd look for. No pitch — just the read. [booking link]

**4.4 When someone tells you something about their child**

Reflect it back in one line — what it's evidence of, in plain words — then:

> That's exactly the kind of thing I mean. If you want, send me a photo of the last report card and give me fifteen minutes; I'll show you what I'd look for in it next to what you just told me. [booking link]

*This is Handoff B. The fifteen minutes with a real report card is a small, honest version of
the exchange the product sells.*

**4.5 When someone asks the price**

> US$597 a year, per child — and that's the whole year: the first Hidden Report Card, then every update as you add new report cards, assessments, and what you notice at home. Founding families this fall get 40% off the first year.
>
> Everything's at truermeasure.com. If you'd rather talk it through first, fifteen minutes: [booking link]

*State only the 40% first-year discount. The renewal discount is under review in Stripe

## 6. Voice checklist — for whoever makes the next pack

Ten checks before a card or script goes out. The EA can run these without the founder.

1. One observation. If there are two ideas, it's two cards.
2. It's about the child or the form — never about Truer Measure — except in pins and §4.
3. No "unlock," "potential," "transform," "game-changer," "journey," "empower," "not just X but Y."
4. No number or "research shows" unless it's in `qualities-research-notes.md` with a source.
5. No child is nameable. No age-plus-town-plus-trait combinations. Nothing from the three
   families without written consent (D-4).
6. Nothing that diagnoses or predicts. "Evidence of," not "means she is."
7. A next step is never rewritten as a deficit.
8. A question beats a claim when the point is the parent's own noticing.
9. Short lines. Read it aloud; if you breathe mid-sentence, break it.
10. The URL appears only in pins, bios, and §4.3 / §4.5. Everywhere else, the keyword.
