## The guide — copy

### How to read your child's report card comment

*What it can say. What it can't. What to write down before the meeting.*

Jolene Gaudet · former school principal · truermeasure.com

---

**Start with what the comment is.**

A few lines. Often around two hundred characters. Written by someone who is writing twenty-five
of them the same week, inside rules about what can and can't be said, sometimes from a bank of
approved phrases. It describes your child in one room, doing what that room asked, as seen by
one adult who was also teaching.

That isn't a criticism of the teacher. It's a description of the form. Read it as a form and
it becomes useful. Read it as a verdict and it becomes noise.

---

**Three things the comment can tell you — reliably**

1. **What the teacher saw most often.** Not what your child is; what showed up most in that
   room, this term. That's real information about that environment.
2. **What the curriculum was asking for.** The comment is written against a list. If the list
   didn't include something, the comment can't mention it — no matter how much of it your child
   showed.
3. **What the teacher chose to name.** With two hundred characters and a bank of phrases, every
   word is a choice. When a teacher spends some of that space on something specific — a moment,
   a behaviour, a *when* — that is the part to underline.

---

**Five things it can't tell you**

- Which of three very different children "works well with others" describes: the leader, the
  peacemaker, or the one who has learned to disappear in a group.
- Whether your child is confident or compliant. On the form, they look identical.
- What she does when nobody is asking. The form only sees assigned work.
- Whether his curiosity is growing or shrinking. One term can't show a direction.
- The strength that has appeared for three years, from three teachers, in three different
  phrasings. Each comment saw one year. Nobody wrote down the pattern.

Every one of those you can see from home. None of them has a line on the form.

---

**Four ways to read it**

**1. Read the verb, not the adjective.**
"Continues to develop" is motion. "Is beginning to" is motion. "Needs to" is a next step — it
means the thing is being worked on. It is not a deficit, and it is not a prediction. Don't
translate it into one.

**2. For every "needs to," ask: what was she doing instead?**
"Needs to stay on task" describes an absence. What was present? Talking to whom, about what?
Drawing what? Watching what? The answer is usually more interesting than the comment, and it's
the part worth writing down.

**3. Put it beside last year's.**
Same word from a different teacher: that's a pattern, not an opinion. Different words for the
same behaviour: also a pattern — the wording changed, the child didn't. If you have three years
of report cards, read the comments in one sitting. Most parents never have. It's a different
document read that way.

**4. Find what's missing.**
Take one minute and list what you know about your child that appears nowhere on the page. The
thing he builds for hours because nobody told him to. The way she knows when she needs quiet.
The question he keeps asking. That list is not a gap in your child. It's a gap in the form —
and it's the record only you can keep.

---

**Before the meeting — write down three things**

Not for the teacher. For you. One line each, tonight.

1. **One thing your child did this month that nobody assigned.**
2. **One thing that has shown up for two years or more** — in any setting, from anyone.
3. **One question you want answered with an example, not a rating.**

Bring the page. You'll find you're listening for different things.

---

**Two questions to ask the teacher**

- *"Can you give me an example of a time you saw that?"* — for anything in the comment, good or
  hard. A rating becomes a moment. Moments are evidence; ratings are summaries of evidence you
  haven't seen.
- *"What does she do when the task turns out to be harder than she expected?"* — the single
  most revealing thing a classroom sees that a form can't hold.

just made the first exchange without paying anything. A parent who replies with what she
wrote is the early adopter from the value record. Track the second number, not the first.
