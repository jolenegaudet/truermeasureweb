# The commitment — one customer, one problem, ten paying families

*2026-09-08. This page is the validation north star. Everything else in this folder serves it or waits behind it. It is written as a decision, not a discussion: the choice is made below with the runner-up named, so the founder can ratify or overrule in one word. Once ratified, nothing here moves until ten families have paid.*

---

## 1. The customer

**A parent of a child in grades 1–6 who already knows the report card is not the whole story — and has started writing things down.**

Recognisable in one question: *"Do you keep notes about your child that school has never seen?"* If the answer is yes — a notes app, a strategy book, photos with captions, a folder — they are the customer. If no, they are not, yet.

Three more things true of them:
- They have **at least two years of report cards** for this child. (One year has no pattern in it; the product's read needs a line, not a point.)
- They have **a school conversation coming** in the next ninety days — a report card, a conference, a decision about a program or support.
- They can pay US$597 without a conversation with themselves about it.

**Where they are:** New Brunswick, through the founder's network and its forwarding layer; South Florida, through the collaborator's network and the founder's own presence there through November.

**Why grades 1–6 for validation when the market is K–12.** The market stays K–12 — that ruling stands. The *first ten* need to be the parents for whom the pain is sharpest and the demonstration cleanest: young enough that the parent still holds most of the record, old enough to have two years of it. Widening comes after the tenth payment, not before.

## 2. The problem

**"The report card doesn't match the child I know — and when it counts, I have nothing in my hands but my memory."**

In their words, from the threads:
- *"It doesn't match up with my kid's skill set… I know I'm not delusional on this."*
- *"I was blindsided at the conference."*
- *"When something is written down, it counts."*
- *"I'm sad they didn't have anything positive to say in any of the comment sections."*

One sentence, StoryBrand-shaped: **The school has a written account of your child. You don't. And the school's is incomplete by design.**

**The runner-up, and why not.** *"I don't know what to do with this report card"* — the decoding problem — has as much evidence, and the strategies (F07) serve it. It loses because the first problem is *demonstrable in thirty seconds with the parent's own material* — put the report card beside what they wrote down — and the second needs the product to explain itself. Demonstration sells the first ten. Decoding is the second ten.

## 3. The three features — and only three

Everything the customer is told about the product, in the order it matters to them. All three exist or are ratified; none is a promise of future work.

| # | Feature, in the parent's terms | What it is in the build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Everything about your child's learning in one place** — every report card, every assessment, every teacher's note, and what you see at home. Add a photo. Add a coach's comment. Add what your child wants kept. | F02 — built. Upload, observations, professional documents, the record view |
| 2 | **The Hidden Report Card** — a written account of your child that reads the school's record beside yours, across the years, and says what recurs, what's changed, and what it's evidence of. Every sentence traces back to something you gave it. Not a grade, not a diagnosis. | F03 (the read) + F04 (the report) + F06 (the founder's review of every first report) |
| 3 | **It keeps going** — add the next report card, the next moment, and ask for an update when you're ready. What's Changed, not a new report. Six a year included; extra updates US$10. | F05 — ratified; the counter is a story to build |

Not a feature for the first ten, however true: the research behind it, the traceability rule, the founder's review, French, the community, the circle. Those are answers to questions, not the pitch.

## 4. The offer

**The Hidden Report Card, US$597 a year per child. Founding Families: the first 40, 40% off the first year with FOUNDING40.** Public price, Stripe checkout, as the site now shows.

Only a payment counts. No free pilots, no "try it and tell me," no "I'll pay when the report is done." A parent who wants to see one first is shown the demonstration (§6) — not given the product.

Learn from the Room and the Inner Circle are **not sold** until the tenth family has paid. They stay on the site as waitlists. Selling them now is the generalist's escape hatch.

## 5. The number, the date, and the rule

- **Ten paying families by 30 November 2026.** Report cards land in early November (Florida) and late November (New Brunswick); that is the buying window, and it closes with the month.
- **Counted only from profile matches** (§1). A payment from someone outside the profile is welcome, banked, and not counted.
- **Weekly:** conversations started · matched · offered · paid. If offers aren't converting, the problem is the pitch. If conversations aren't matching, the problem is where you're looking. If matches aren't converting to offers, the problem is the price or the proof.
- **The rule:** at ten, widen — the second problem, the second ten, then the Room. Short of ten on 30 November, change the customer or the problem — **not the features**. Building more is how the generalist avoids finding out.

## 6. The demonstration — what closes

Not a description. A read. In the fifteen-minute conversation, the parent brings one report card and one thing they wrote down. You put them side by side and read them the way you'd read them for a family: what the comment can hold, what it can't, what the parent's note is evidence of, the question to ask the teacher. That's the product, done once, on their child, live.

The Thursday Read on social is the same demonstration in public, on strangers' lines. The DM word BEYOND is how a stranger asks for it privately.

## 7. Only talk to matches — the screen

Three questions, asked in the DM or at the door, before any offer:

1. *"What grade is your child in?"* — 1 to 6 for the first ten.
2. *"Do you keep notes about your child that school has never seen — anything, anywhere?"* — yes, or not yet the customer.
3. *"Is there a report card or a meeting coming up in the next couple of months?"* — yes, or not now.

Two of three: continue. One or none: thank them, send the series, don't offer. Every conversation logged with the three answers, so the profile can be corrected by evidence if it's wrong.

## 9. The pitch deck — ten slides, one story

For the founder, the collaborator, and anyone asked to help find the ten. Built from this page; nothing in it that isn't above.

1. **The moment.** A parent across the table from a teacher, a report card between them, everything they know about their child in their memory. *"When something is written down, it counts."*
2. **The customer.** §1, in one sentence, with the recognising question.
3. **The problem.** §2 — the school has a written account of your child and you don't; theirs is incomplete by design. Three parent quotes.
4. **Why the form is incomplete — by design.** Report cards were built for reliability and sorting, not description (Part 7's history, one slide: 1848, 1912, A–F). Everything a parent wants to know is variance; the form removes it.
5. **What we built.** The three features, §3, in the parent's terms.
6. **What it looks like.** One anonymized excerpt from a delivered Hidden Report Card, with consent (D-4) — or, until consent, the demonstration described (§6).
7. **What already happened.** Three founding families; three of three came back with more, unasked — a psychologist's assessment, a strengths inventory written on a parent's own initiative, a family sorting qualities together. *"This made me cry. I'll need to read it again."* (with consent).
8. **The offer and the price.** §4.
9. **The validation.** Ten paying, profile-matched, by 30 November; the weekly numbers; the rule at ten and short of ten (§5).
10. **The ask.** Names. Every person who sees this deck is asked for one parent who matches §1.

## 10. What is deliberately not happening until the tenth payment

Each of these is a real idea. Each is also a way to keep options open. The list is here so the pull toward them is visible when it comes.
